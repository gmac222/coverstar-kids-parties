How to Plan a Kids' Birthday Party in Liverpool

By CoverStar Kids Parties Team · 15 November 2026 · 5 min read

Planning a children's party is one of those jobs that looks small until you start it. The good news is that it breaks down into a handful of decisions, and if you make them in the right order the rest falls into place. This guide walks through the timeline we see work best for Liverpool families, with the local details that national party guides tend to miss.

Start With Three Decisions

Almost everything else depends on these, so settle them first:

  1. Who is coming. A rough number is enough for now. Whole class, half a dozen close friends, or family plus a few mates. The number drives the venue, the budget and the format.
  2. Roughly when. The actual birthday, the nearest weekend, or a date in the school holidays. Liverpool schools do not all share identical term dates, so if guests come from more than one school, check both calendars before you print invitations.
  3. At home or out. A home party is cheaper on paper but costs you a full day of setting up, hosting and clearing. A venue or activity party costs more per child but comes with staff, structure and someone else doing the tidying.

The Planning Timeline

WhenWhat to do
6 to 8 weeks beforeShortlist venues and check availability. Popular Saturday afternoon slots across Liverpool go quickly, especially in the run-up to Christmas and the May and October half terms. Pay the deposit to hold your date.
4 to 5 weeks beforeConfirm the guest list with your child. Send invitations with a clear RSVP date about two weeks before the party.
3 weeks beforeOrder or plan the cake. Decide on food: bring your own, order in, or use what the venue provides. Ask parents about allergies on the RSVP.
1 to 2 weeks beforeChase RSVPs. Confirm final numbers with the venue and check what time you can arrive to set up. Buy or make party bags if you are doing them.
2 days beforePack a party box: candles, lighter, knife, napkins, plates, a bin bag, phone charger, wet wipes, spare plasters. Write the address and phone number of the venue on the invitation reminder you send to parents.
On the dayArrive early, brief whoever is helping you, and give yourself one job: enjoy it. Delegate photos to another adult.

Choosing a Liverpool Venue

Liverpool has a wide spread of party options, from soft play centres and trampoline parks on the retail parks to city centre experiences you cannot get elsewhere. When you compare them, ask the same questions of each:

  • Is the price per child or a flat package? What is the minimum and maximum group size?
  • How long is the slot, and does it include time for food and cake?
  • Is the space private or shared with the public?
  • What do the children take home? A party bag of sweets, or something they will keep?
  • Where do parents wait, and how many adults need to stay?

At our own recording studio parties the answers are: flat package pricing from £199 for 8 children, slots of 1.5 or 2 hours including a party room for food, a private studio, a recorded song on a vinyl-style CD plus a download, and a parents' lounge with a bar. At least one parent or guardian stays throughout. If you are weighing up other kinds of venue, our guide to party ideas beyond soft play runs through the main categories.

Getting There: City Centre Logistics

If you book anywhere in the city centre, give parents the practical details in advance so nobody arrives flustered.

  • Parking. Our studio is at 24a Arrad Street in the Georgian Quarter (L7 7BP). On-street parking on Hope Street and the Metropolitan Cathedral car park are both about a two-minute walk away. Weekend parking in the Georgian Quarter is usually easier than around Liverpool ONE.
  • Trains. Liverpool Lime Street and Liverpool Central are both a 10 to 15 minute walk from the Georgian Quarter, which suits families coming in from the Wirral, Southport or Ormskirk on Merseyrail.
  • Drop-off. Agree a single drop-off and collection point with parents. Children spilling out across a city pavement is the most stressful ten minutes of any party.

Food Without the Fuss

Children at a party eat less than you think and far more sugar than you plan. A simple spread works: a savoury option, fruit, crisps, and the cake. If your venue lets you bring your own food, as we do, that keeps costs down and means you can cater for allergies yourself. Many of our parents order pizza to arrive while the CDs are being printed; tell the venue in advance so it lands at the right moment and does not interrupt the activity.

Party Bags: Worth It?

Party bags are the most-skipped item on modern party lists and few children notice. If the party itself produces a keepsake, such as a craft they made, a photo, or in our case a recording of their song, that does the job of the party bag with far less plastic. If you do want bags, one decent item beats five cheap ones.

A Few Things Experienced Parents Do

  • Keep the party to two hours. It is long enough to feel like an event and short enough that nobody melts down.
  • Invite an even number if the activity involves pairing up.
  • Put a second adult in charge of the birthday child's gifts and cards so they do not get separated.
  • Ask the venue when the best moment for cake is. Doing it at the natural break in the activity keeps the energy up.
  • Take one group photo before anything starts, while everyone still looks tidy.
