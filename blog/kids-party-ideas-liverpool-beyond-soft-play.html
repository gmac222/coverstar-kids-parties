Kids' Party Ideas in Liverpool Beyond Soft Play

By CoverStar Kids Parties Team · 15 November 2026 · 4 min read

There is nothing wrong with soft play. For toddlers and infants it is close to perfect. The problem is that by Year 2 or 3 most children in Liverpool have been to every soft play centre and trampoline park within half an hour of home, often several times each. If you are hunting for something different, Liverpool has more options than its retail parks suggest.

Creative and Performance Parties

Record a song in a real studio

This is what we do, so we will declare the bias up front. A group of children rehearse and record a pop song with a professional sound engineer in a working Liverpool recording studio, with a lyric screen and backing track so nobody needs to be a confident singer. The birthday child can do solo takes, everyone sings the chorus together, and each child leaves with the recording on a vinyl-style CD and a download. It suits ages 7 to 16, works equally well for mixed groups, and the recording is something families genuinely keep. See how a CoverStar party works.

Pottery painting and craft studios

Liverpool has several paint-your-own-pottery and craft studios that host parties. Quieter than an activity party, good for children who prefer making to running, and everyone takes home what they made. Allow for a return trip or postage if pieces need glazing and firing.

Theatre and drama workshops

Several Liverpool drama schools and theatres run party workshops where a practitioner leads games and a mini performance. A good fit for children who love dressing up and being the centre of attention.

Out and About in Liverpool

Museums on the waterfront

The World Museum, Museum of Liverpool and the Maritime Museum are free to enter, which makes a self-organised museum trail party one of the cheapest options in the city. Set a scavenger hunt, finish with cake at a nearby café. Best for smaller, well-behaved groups with plenty of adults.

Beaches and parks

Crosby beach with the Antony Gormley iron men, Formby's dunes and pinewoods, and Sefton Park with its Palm House are all free and a short drive or Merseyrail trip from the city. Great for summer birthdays with a picnic and games. Have a wet-weather backup.

Stadium tours

For a football-mad child, a stadium tour at Anfield is a memorable birthday treat for a small group, especially combined with lunch afterwards. Check minimum ages and group terms with the club.

The Mersey Ferry and the Beatles

A ferry across the Mersey followed by the Beatles Story at the Albert Dock is a proper Liverpool day out for older children who are into music. If your child is a budding musician, pair the Beatles theme with recording a Beatles song at a studio party and you have a theme that runs through the whole day.

Active Alternatives to Soft Play

  • Climbing and bouldering centres run children's parties with instructors. Good for ages 6 and up with a competitive streak.
  • Swimming parties at council leisure centres are often good value and include a party room. Check lifeguard ratios and adult requirements.
  • Football and multi-sport parties on 3G pitches with a coach. Simple, cheap per head, and parents can watch from the sideline.
  • Bowling and arcades remain a reliable crowd-pleaser from about age 8, and most alleys offer party packages with food.
  • Escape rooms for ages 10 and up. Most rooms take 4 to 8 players, so book two rooms for a bigger group.

Food-Led Parties

Pizza-making and cupcake-decorating parties have become popular because they solve two problems at once: the activity and the food are the same thing. Several Liverpool restaurants and cookery schools offer them. They suit smaller groups and children who like a bit of mess.

Questions to Ask Any Venue

  • Is the space private for the party or shared with the public?
  • How many adults need to stay, and where do they wait?
  • Can we bring our own food and cake?
  • What does each child take home?
  • What happens if a child does not want to join in?

That last question matters more than parents expect. At our studio the answer is that nobody is forced to sing solo. Shy children sing in the group takes, and the sound engineer mixes everyone together so the finished track sounds great whoever was loudest.
